# Strategic & MBA Frameworks Reference Guide

This reference outlines the exact analytical frameworks and operational rubrics used across the 6 stages of `/industry-analysis`.

---

## 1. DuPont Analysis (Stage 1: Operator Unit Economics)

Deconstructs an operator's Return on Equity (ROE) to diagnose the structural source of financial vulnerability:

$$\text{ROE} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Revenue}} \right) \times \left( \frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Assets}} \right) \times \left( \frac{\text{Assets}}{\text{Equity}} \right)$$

$$\text{ROE} = \text{Net Profit Margin} \times \text{Asset Turnover} \times \text{Financial Leverage}$$

### Diagnostic Archetypes:
1. **The Capital-Trapped Asset Rentier (e.g., Dutch Dairy Farms, Heavy Machine Yards):**
   * *Profile:* Extremely high assets (land/equipment), low asset turnover ($<0.2\times$), thin profit margin ($1\%–4\%$).
   * *Strategic Implication:* High paper net worth but near-zero cash liquidity. Highly vulnerable to interest rate hikes and input inflation. Will **never** buy discretionary software out of operating income.
2. **The Working-Capital Squeezed Operator (e.g., Construction Subcontractors, Freight Haulers):**
   * *Profile:* Modest assets, high turnover, razor-thin margin ($2\%–3\%$), negative cash conversion cycle drag.
   * *Strategic Implication:* Desperately cash-flow constrained. Any tool that accelerates cash collection by 14 days or halts invoice retention can extract premium pricing.

## 2. Porter's Five Forces & The Value Chain (Stage 2: Industry Structure)

### Value Chain Hourglass Mapping:
Map gross margins across the 5 primary activities (Inbound Logistics, Operations, Outbound Logistics, Marketing & Sales, Service) and support activities:

```
[ Upstream Monopolies / Oligopolies ]  --> IP, Genetics, Specialized Automation (Gross Margins: 30%–50%)
                 │
                 ▼
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│           THE SQUEEZE ZONE           │  --> Fragmented primary producers / processors
│   (Margins: 0%–5%, 100% Risk)        │      absorb biological, weather, commodity downside.
└────────────────┬─────────────────────┘
                 │
                 ▼
[ Downstream Gatekeepers / Oligopolies]--> Supermarkets, Master Wholesalers (High ROIC, Negative Working Capital)
```

### The 5 Forces Audit Checklist:
1. **Threat of New Entrants:** Are there regulatory quotas, capital moats, or statutory licenses limiting new operators?
2. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers:** Do suppliers possess patent lock-in (e.g., F1 hybrid seeds, specialized engine diagnostics)?
3. **Bargaining Power of Buyers:** Is the buyer base consolidated into an oligopsony (e.g., 3 supermarket chains controlling 80% of retail food)?
4. **Threat of Substitutes:** Can customers switch to alternative materials or synthetic alternatives?
5. **Competitive Rivalry:** Is the middle layer fragmented into thousands of undifferentiated price-takers competing on commodity price?

## 3. Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers (Stage 3: Bottleneck Strategy)

Used to evaluate the structural defensibility of venture hypotheses:

1. **Cornered Resource:** Preferential access to scarce biological strains, proprietary physics/sensor patents, or restricted regulatory licenses.
2. **Switching Costs:** Deep integration into hardware, daily mechanical workflows, or certified compliance ledgers where rip-and-replace costs exceed annual software fees.
3. **Counter-Positioning:** Adopting a business model that an incumbent cannot copy without cannibalizing their core high-margin revenue (e.g., conflict-free advisory vs. feed/chemical sales).
4. **Scale Economies:** Amortizing fixed R&D or capital equipment across multi-farm/multi-operator volume (e.g., contractor machinery aggregation).
5. **Network Effects:** Data sharing where each additional operator's telemetry improves the predictive accuracy of the compliance or yield model.
6. **Process Power:** Proprietary operational workflows or manufacturing speeds that cannot be matched by competitors.
7. **Branding:** Established, certified trust badges (e.g., Beter Leven, SKAL, DLG certification).

## 4. Principal-Agent Theory (Stage 6: Channel & Distribution Strategy)

Used to diagnose misaligned incentives in the distribution channel and formulate counter-positioning wedges:

### The "Free" Advice Audit:
* **The Question:** *Who currently advises the primary operator, and how does that advisor get paid?*
* **The Conflict:** If the advisor receives salary, commissions, or corporate bonuses from selling consumable input volume (chemicals, feed, spare parts, building supplies), their advisory output is structurally biased toward over-consumption.
* **The Counter-Positioning Wedge:**
  1. Position your product as the **unbiased, independent fiduciary tool**.
  2. Partner with the independent professional layer (accountants, independent consultants, legal advisors) whose billable hours increase when they expose and eliminate supplier waste.

## 5. Agentic Value Migration & Agent-Native Ideation Vectors (Stages 2 & 3)

### Christensen's Law of Conservation of Attractive Profits (Agentic Corollary):
When cognitive synthesis, code generation, and routine clerical work commoditize to near-zero marginal cost, profits shift to:
1. **Physical Actuation & Hardware Interfacing:** Controlling the machines and physical bottlenecks (atoms > bits).
2. **Proprietary Non-Public Data ("Dark Data"):** Real-time sensor telemetry, closed ERP API feeds (e.g. JoinData), and proprietary physical logs inaccessible to web-scraping foundation models.
3. **Statutory Liability Underwriting:** Algorithms cannot be sued or penalized; certified legal envelopes and liability-backed compliance ledgers capture massive willingness-to-pay.

### The 4 Agent-Native Generative Vectors:
1. **Continuous High-Frequency Micro-Steering:** Replaces retrospective annual/quarterly human reviews with 24/7 autonomous closed-loop adjustments.
2. **Machine-as-Customer / M2M Infrastructure:** Protocols and clearinghouses built for autonomous agents transacting directly with other agents without human UIs.
3. **The Fiduciary & Liability Shield:** Cryptographic audit trails that programmatically verify compliance and absorb liability for agent actions in regulated industries.
4. **Post-Seat Value Capture:** Business models strictly designed around outcome-share (% of fines avoided, cash unlocked) or asset-SLA pricing, completely immune to the collapse of human seat counts.

## 6. The Agentic Bridge & Moat Accumulation Rubric (The Trojan Horse Strategy)

A venture must be rewarded if it builds a practical, human-friendly utility today that **quietly accumulates structural moats for the agentic era**:

### The 4 Bridge Moats (Scored 0 to 4 in Stage 4):
1. **Proprietary "Dark Data" (The Calibration Moat):** Does daily usage compile non-public, high-frequency operational telemetry that open-web foundation models cannot scrape? (e.g. real-world biological responses, soil/chemical interactions, machine failure logs).
2. **Captive Node Reach & OAuth Permissions (The Access Moat):** Does it secure authorized API credentials, OAuth tokens, and trusted commercial connections to the actual operating nodes? (Compute is abundant; authenticated access is scarce).
3. **Statutory System of Record (The Authority Moat):** Does it become the authoritative legal/financial ledger of record where historical audit trails live? (Autonomous agents cannot act legally without an authoritative ground truth).
4. **Proven Economic Outcomes (The Fiduciary Moat):** Does it establish a track record of hard cash saved/earned that earns unbreakable customer inertia and trust?

### Strategic Decision Rule:
* **Score $\ge 3$ out of 4:** **🟢 ALPHA REINFORCE.** Even if the initial product is an unglamorous B2B workflow or hybrid tool, it is an elite venture because it builds the insurmountable data and access runway for the agentic flip.
* **Score $\le 1$ out of 4:** **🔴 KILL as AI-Transient.** The venture is a temporary feature that will be commoditized as soon as generic autonomous agents enter the domain.
